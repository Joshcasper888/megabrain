**When:** Tue 9/30, about 5:00 PM  **Phone:** (207) 206-5065
**Business:** Everest207 Landscaping LLC, 1632 Post Road, Wells, ME. About 5 years in business.
**Services:** landscaping, patios and hardscape, retaining walls, walkways and steps, excavation, hydroseed and sod, mulching, stump and tree work, drainage.
**Reputation:** 5.0 on Angi (100% recommended), 4.48 across 23 reviews. A Google review says "The quality of the patio was beyond my expectations."
**Area:** Wells, Kennebunk, Ogunquit, York, Sanford, southern Maine.

## His current ads (the problem)
- The same ad is running 6 times, and the headline is just "Everest207 Landscaping."
- There's no offer, no season or urgency, and no reason to tap.
- Six copies of one ad means Meta splits his money instead of testing anything.

## Before the call (5 minutes)
- Have the 3 preview ads open on your phone. They're in Google Drive ("Everest207 preview ads") or in the repo under spec-ads/everest207-ad1..3.png.
- Have the agreement ready to send ("Service Agreement: 3 Options" in Drive).
- Have the onboarding guide ready ("2. Client Onboarding Guide").
- Have a pen ready to write down his answers.

## Call flow (about 15 minutes)

### 1. Open (1 min)
"Hey Michael, it's Josh from ApexLeads. Thanks for making time. Did you get a chance to look at my email? ... Great. I've got the ads I'd run for you ready. Want to go through those, then figure out which setup works best for you?"

### 2. Ask first, then listen (3–4 min)
Ask these, write the answers down, and use his own words later.
- "What kind of job do you want more of right now: cleanups, patios and hardscape, or something else?"
- "What's a typical patio or hardscape job worth to you?" (It shows him what one extra job is worth. With the flat fee, he never has to answer this.)
- "How are most of your jobs coming in right now? Referrals, Angi, Facebook?"
- "When you've boosted or run ads before, what happened?"
- "How fast can you usually call someone back when a lead comes in?"
- "Is there a busy season you're trying to fill, or a slow one?"

### 3. Show the ads (4 min)
Text him the 3 images while you're on the phone, or tell him to check his email.
- **Ad 1, Fall cleanup:** "This one is for right now. People want their yards done before the freeze, and it fills your October and November."
- **Ad 2, Patio and hardscape for spring:** "This is the money one. We book patio jobs this fall and winter, so you start spring with a full schedule instead of waiting for the phone to ring. It uses your own Google review."
- **Ad 3, Reviews and trust:** "You've got a 5.0 on Angi. This one puts that front and center for people comparing landscapers."
- "Each ad has a button that opens a quick form: what they need, their town, name and phone. It goes straight to your phone."

### 4. The 3 options (3 min)
"Like I mentioned, you pick how you want to pay me."
- **Per lead, $30 each.** You only pay for real leads, and bad leads get taken off the bill. That usually comes to about $600–900 a month.
- **15% of jobs from the ads.** You only pay when an ad turns into a paying job. You'd just send me a short list each month.
- **Flat $600 a month.** One set price, and you never share your numbers.
- All three are month to month with no contract. Ad spend goes on his own card, about $20–30 a day to start.

**How to steer him:**
- If he's worried about wasting money, recommend **per lead**.
- If he's busy and wants it simple, recommend **flat $600**. This one is best for you.
- If he mainly wants big patio jobs, 15% can pay you the most, but you have to rely on his list of which jobs came from the ads.

### 5. Close (2 min)
"Which one feels right to you?"
Once he picks:
1. "Perfect. I'll email you the one-page agreement tonight with that option checked."
2. "After you sign, the setup takes about 10 minutes. You add me as a partner on your Facebook page and ad account, and I'll send step-by-step instructions."
3. "I build everything, you approve the ads, and we can be live within a few days."
4. Ask: "What's the best email for you, and can you send me 5–10 of your best job photos, especially patios?"

## Objections and what to say

**"I need to think about it."**
"Totally fair. What part are you unsure about: the price, the ads, or whether it'll work? ... If it helps, start with per lead. If nothing comes in, you owe me nothing."

**"$600 is a lot."**
"One patio job covers that many times over. But if you'd rather not commit to a flat number, per lead means you only pay for what comes in."

**"I already run my own ads."**
"You do, and that puts you ahead of most. But right now it's one ad copied 6 times with no offer, so Facebook has nothing to test. I'd turn that same budget into ads that give people a reason to call."

**"I don't want to share my numbers."**
"Then go flat monthly or per lead. You never share revenue or job values with me."

**"Can you guarantee leads?"**
"Nobody honest can guarantee it. What I can promise is new ads, real testing every week, and a weekly report so you see exactly what's happening. And with per lead, you're only paying when it works."

**"Will I lose control of my page or account?"**
"No. You own everything. I'm added as a partner and you can remove me with one click. I never need your password, and nothing goes live without your OK."

**"What if the leads are junk?"**
"Every ad has a short form: do you own the home, what do you need, your town. That filters out most tire-kickers. Under per lead, anything bad gets taken off your bill."

**"Winter's coming, isn't it slow?"**
"That's exactly why this is the right time. Fall cleanups now, then we book spring patio jobs all winter while your competitors go quiet."
